# Risk register

Divergences between postfiatd and rippled 3.1.2, judged by impact on
whether the two implementations agree on ledgers, validate the same
transactions, and stay in sync. Severity is judged by impact on
consensus agreement and on a node's ability to keep up with its peers.

| Risk | Severity | Risk | Evidence / mitigation in the pinned code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K-01 | HIGH | Lower fork-safety margin: a 67% quorum tolerates 33 Byzantine validators in 100 where rippled tolerates 20, and two UNLs may safely share less overlap. | 67% quorum (R-03, R-04, F-01); threshold table; scenario S-01. Mitigation: keep the UNL overlap high and treat the 67% quorum as a testnet setting — the code itself notes mainnet should revert to 80% (`ValidatorList.cpp:1899-1900`). |
| K-02 | HIGH | Vote pseudo-transactions make postfiatd ledgers unreplayable by rippled: a ledger containing ttVALIDATOR_VOTE or ltVALIDATOR_VOTE_STATS cannot be validated or replayed by the reference implementation. | Vote tracking (R-31, R-32, R-33, F-02); scenarios S-06/S-08 (incompatible). Mitigation: do not mix implementations on one network; treat the vote ledger objects as postfiatd-only state. |
| K-03 | HIGH | Exclusion enforcement forks a mixed network: the same transaction is rejected by postfiatd and applied by rippled, so the two implementations build different ledgers from the same input. | Preflight exclusion (R-34, F-03); scenario S-07 (diverged). Mitigation: run a single implementation per network; if exclusion matters, every validator must run postfiatd. |
| K-04 | MEDIUM | Close-time divergence: the median and the mean disagree whenever peer close times spread, so the two implementations adjust their clocks differently after the same round. | Median vs mean (R-06, F-04); scenario S-03. Mitigation: keep validator clocks tightly synchronized; the median is the safer side under skew. |
| K-05 | MEDIUM | Close-time tie-break flip: on an exact tie the two implementations pick different consensus close times, so their next proposals differ. | Tie-break (R-07, F-05); scenario S-04. Mitigation: none in the code; ties are rare and the next round re-converges. |
| K-06 | MEDIUM | Pause-phase divergence: a network where 70% of validators moved on pauses in rippled and proceeds in postfiatd, so the two implementations progress at different rates under partial outages. | Pause thresholds (R-08, F-06); scenario S-05. Mitigation: do not mix implementations; expect slower progress on the rippled side during partial outages. |
| K-07 | MEDIUM | 36 flipped amendment defaults change fresh-network behavior: features rippled leaves off by default are on by default in postfiatd. | Defaults flipped (R-38, F-07). Mitigation: audit the amendment set of any fresh network against the rippled defaults before going live. |
| K-08 | MEDIUM | Parse caps reject inputs rippled accepts: a 600-element JSON array or a non-canonical validation is rejected by postfiatd and accepted by rippled. | Parse hardening (R-28, R-44, R-10, F-08); scenario S-12. Mitigation: keep submitted transactions inside the 512-element array cap and canonical field order. |
| K-09 | MEDIUM | Unbounded manifest cache in rippled: a manifest flood grows the cache without limit, while postfiatd caps it at 1,000. | Manifest cap (R-15, F-09); scenario S-13 (degraded). Mitigation: run postfiatd, or monitor manifest-cache growth on rippled nodes. |
| K-10 | MEDIUM | Hardening gaps in rippled: out-of-range SHAMap depths, malformed replay responses, and overflowing balance arithmetic are clamped or returned as errors in postfiatd and assert, wrap, or conflate in rippled. | Hardening (R-23, R-24, R-25, R-39, R-40, R-41, F-10); scenarios S-10/S-11. Mitigation: prefer the hardened implementation for nodes exposed to untrusted peers. |
| K-11 | MEDIUM | Exclusion lists cap at 100 addresses: a list that fills cannot express more exclusions, and the cap is enforced silently at the list level. | Exclusion cap (R-36); `Protocol.h:238`. Mitigation: rotate exclusions out when the list fills; monitor the exclusion count through the exclusion_info RPC. |
| K-12 | LOW | Genesis incompatibility: networks 2025/2026 start from a different genesis account with earliest sequence 1, so no rippled node can sync a postfiatd genesis ledger. | Genesis (R-19, R-20, F-11); scenario S-09. Mitigation: none — the networks are separate by design; do not point a rippled node at a postfiatd network. |
| K-13 | LOW | Pin uncertainty: the exact fork commit within the 5-commit plateau (2026-03-11–12) is indistinguishable because postfiatd overwrote the version strings that differ between those commits. | Pin method (`docs/methodology.md`); R-01, R-02. Mitigation: treat 3.1.2 as the reference revision, not a single commit, for any claim that depends on the exact fork point. |
| K-14 | LOW | Vote tracking adds per-ledger work: doVoting runs for every ledger with no measured performance cost in this pack. | Vote tracking (R-14); gap G-02. Mitigation: measure doVoting cost under load before enabling vote tracking on a busy validator. |
